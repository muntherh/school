1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the proposed requirements for a School Disciplinary Management System (SDMS). The system will provide an authenticated web portal through which authorized school staff can document student-related incidents, review and manage cases, record decisions and support interventions, monitor follow-ups, and export authorized records for administrative or official reporting.
1.2 Background
The school needs a consistent, searchable, and auditable method of documenting disciplinary incidents and the actions taken in response. The proposed system replaces or supplements scattered manual records with a central backend database and controlled workflows. CSV files are intended for authorized reporting and data exchange, not as the primary system of record.
1.3 Objectives
Maintain consistent digital records of reported incidents and subsequent decisions.
Allow teachers to submit and track complaints they have reported.
Enable the principal to approve staff accounts and oversee all cases.
Enable the school counsellor to review authorized cases and document interventions and follow-ups.
Provide a reliable history of actions, changes, and case outcomes.
Generate filtered CSV exports for authorized school and government reporting.
Protect sensitive student information through role-based access and appropriate security controls.
1.4 Scope
The initial release is expected to include:
Staff authentication, account approval, and role-based authorization.
Student record lookup and incident submission.
Principal review, case assignment, status tracking, and action recording.
Counsellor case access, intervention notes, and follow-up tracking.
Dashboards, search, filters, case history, and CSV export.
Audit logging, basic validation, and administrative configuration.
The system is not intended to make automated disciplinary decisions. Decisions remain the responsibility of authorized school personnel and must follow school policy and applicable regulations.

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
The SDMS will be a browser-based full-stack application. The frontend will communicate with a backend API. The backend will authenticate users, enforce permissions, validate requests, manage case workflows, and persist data in a relational database. CSV exports will be generated from authorized database queries.
2.2 Product Functions
Authenticate staff and restrict access until the required account approval is complete.
Create, view, update, and track incident records according to permissions and case status.
Allow the principal to review, return, assign, approve, or close cases as defined by school policy.
Allow the counsellor to record interventions and follow-up outcomes for authorized cases.
Show dashboards and case lists relevant to each user’s role.
Search and filter cases using permitted fields.
Export authorized datasets to CSV with appropriate filters and safeguards.
Maintain a traceable history of case changes and significant administrative actions.
2.3 Operating Environment
Area
Proposed Requirement
Client
Modern desktop and mobile web browsers supported by the school.
Frontend
Responsive web application; React or Next.js are possible implementation choices.
Backend
REST API or equivalent service; Node.js/Express or Django are possible choices.
Database
Relational database such as PostgreSQL; the final choice is a technical design decision.
Hosting
School-approved hosting environment with backups, access controls, and secure configuration.
Export
CSV download for authorized users; optional future support for other formats subject to approval.
2.4 Design and Implementation Principles
The database is the authoritative record; CSV files are generated exports.
Access is restricted by role and, where necessary, by case assignment or reporting relationship.
Sensitive counselling notes may require stricter permissions than general incident details.
Case decisions and status changes should be traceable and should not be silently overwritten.
The system should support accessible, clear, and responsive user interfaces.

5. Business Rules and Case Workflow
5.1 Proposed Workflow
Stage
Description
1. Staff access
Teacher account is created or requested; principal approves or rejects it.
2. Report submitted
Teacher submits a new incident report. The system creates a unique reference and records the submission.
3. Initial review
The case enters the principal's review queue. The principal can review, request clarification, assign, or take another policy-approved action.
4. Investigation / support
The principal may assign the case to the counsellor or another authorized staff member, according to policy.
5. Decision and intervention
Authorized staff record the approved action, rationale, responsible person, and any intervention or follow-up plan.
6. Follow-up
The responsible staff member records progress and completion of planned follow-ups.
7. Closure
The principal or other authorized role closes the case when the required actions are completed.
8. Reporting
Authorized users search and export appropriate records for school review or approved external reporting.
5.2 Business Rules
A user must be authenticated and active before accessing protected functionality.
A staff account must be approved by the principal before it can access the portal, unless an alternate school-approved onboarding method is selected.
Account approval and incident approval are separate processes.
Every submitted incident must have a unique reference and an identifiable reporting user.
Only authorized roles may change case status, record formal decisions, or close/reopen cases.
Every significant case change must retain its actor and timestamp; previous history must remain available to authorized reviewers.
A case may contain multiple actions, interventions, and follow-up entries.
An incident report must not be treated as proof that the reported allegation is established; the system should distinguish reported facts, review findings, and decisions.
CSV exports must follow the exporter's permissions and the approved purpose for sharing.
The application must not automatically assign punishment or make high-impact disciplinary decisions using AI or reinforcement learning.

6.2 Data Validation
Each entity shall have a unique primary identifier.
References to students, users, cases, and actions shall be validated against existing records.
Required fields shall be checked by the backend before records are saved.
Date and time values shall use a consistent stored format and be displayed in the school's configured time zone.
Free-text fields shall have reasonable length limits and be protected against injection attacks.
The system shall prevent accidental deletion of records required for audit or retention; correction and archival rules must be approved.
6.3 Retention, Archival, and Deletion
The school must define record-retention periods, archival rules, legal holds, and approved deletion procedures before production use. The system should support these policies without allowing ordinary users to erase the audit trail. The SRS does not assume a specific statutory retention period.
7. External Interface Requirements
7.1 User Interface
The interface shall provide clear navigation appropriate to each role.
Forms shall identify required fields and explain validation errors in plain language.
Case lists shall show a reference number, student identifier or approved display name, incident date, status, and permitted summary fields.
Sensitive content should not be exposed in dashboard previews, browser notifications, or URLs.
The interface should be responsive for common desktop, tablet, and mobile screen sizes.
7.2 Software Interfaces
Frontend-to-backend communication shall use authenticated, validated API requests over HTTPS in production.
The database shall be accessible only through the backend or approved database administration channels.
Any future integration with a school information system, identity provider, email, SMS, or Ministry system requires separate interface and security specifications.
No external integration is assumed for the initial release unless stakeholders confirm it.
7.3 File Interface
The system shall generate CSV files using documented headers and a consistent encoding. The export specification must identify the permitted columns, date format, value conventions, and whether identifiers or names are included. Importing CSV data into the live database is not included unless separately approved and specified.

9. Reporting and CSV Export
9.1 Proposed Export Fields
The exact export schema must be approved by the school. A standard administrative export may include the following fields when the requesting user is authorized:
Case reference
Student identifier and approved student display field
Class or grade
Incident date and submission date
Incident category and description, if approved for the report
Reporting staff identifier or approved staff display field
Current case status
Review decision and decision date
Action type, action date, and responsible staff member
Intervention/follow-up status and outcome, only where permitted
Closure date
9.2 Export Controls
The user shall select an approved date range and permitted filters before export.
The system shall apply the same authorization rules to exported data as to on-screen data.
Confidential counselling notes shall be excluded from general exports unless explicit authorization and purpose are established.
The export action shall be logged and a warning shall remind users to handle student data securely.
The school shall define an approved process for sharing exported files with staff or external government organizations.
10. Security, Privacy, and Audit
Use least-privilege access: each role receives only the permissions necessary for its duties.
Enforce authorization in the backend for pages, APIs, individual records, and export fields.
Protect data in transit and use suitable at-rest protection based on hosting and risk assessment.
Maintain an audit trail for access and important changes, including account administration, case decisions, and exports.
Avoid exposing student names or sensitive incident details in URLs, error messages, analytics, or routine notifications.
Define how suspected security incidents, unauthorized access, and accidental disclosures are reported and handled.
Approve data retention, backups, recovery, access review, and account deactivation procedures.
Before external sharing, verify legal authority, recipient, purpose, scope, and minimum required fields.
Conduct security testing before launch and after material changes.
11. Assumptions, Dependencies, and Constraints
11.1 Assumptions
The school will appoint the principal or another authorized representative to approve requirements and policies.
The school can provide an authoritative student list or define how student records will be created and maintained.
The school will define which incident categories, action types, and case statuses are permitted.
Staff will use the system only for legitimate school purposes and follow confidentiality rules.
A school-approved hosting environment and operational owner will be available.
11.2 Dependencies
Confirmation of applicable Ministry requirements and school policies.
Agreement on account onboarding, identity verification, and password recovery.
Agreement on student identifiers and any existing data source.
Availability of hosting, domain, backup, and security arrangements.
Approval of CSV fields and the procedure for external data sharing.
11.3 Constraints
The system handles sensitive information relating to minors and requires heightened confidentiality.
The initial release is a web application; native mobile applications are not assumed.
CSV is an export format and is not the authoritative transactional data store.
Automated punishment decisions and reinforcement-learning-based disciplinary recommendations are not part of the initial release.
12. Out of Scope
Automatic determination of guilt, risk scores, or disciplinary punishment using AI.
Reinforcement learning that changes student interventions or sanctions automatically.
Parent/student portal access unless separately approved.
Direct integration with Ministry systems, student information systems, email, SMS, or national identity systems.
Bulk import or synchronization from CSV into the production database.
Legal determination of compliance; the system supports documentation but does not replace school policy or legal review.
Payroll, attendance, examinations, fees, and other general school ERP functions.
13. Acceptance Criteria
The initial release will be considered ready for stakeholder acceptance when the following conditions are met:
An unapproved staff account cannot access protected system functions, and the principal can approve or reject it.
An approved teacher can submit an incident with required fields and receive a unique reference number.
The teacher can view the status of their own submitted reports but cannot view unauthorized cases.
The principal can see all cases in scope, review them, request clarification, assign cases, record decisions, and close cases according to the approved workflow.
The counsellor can access only authorized cases and record interventions and follow-ups.
The system retains a chronological history of submissions, reviews, decisions, actions, and closures.
Dashboards and filters display correct data for each role.
An authorized user can export a filtered CSV with the approved columns, and unauthorized fields are excluded.
Successful exports and important administrative/case actions are logged.
Security tests verify backend authorization, session handling, and protection of restricted records.
Backup and restoration procedures are documented and tested.
School stakeholders approve the workflow, data fields, retention policy, access matrix, and export process.
